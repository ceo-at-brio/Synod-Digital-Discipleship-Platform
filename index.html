Fulfils Section 1: User Authentication & Onboarding (p. 3–4). Flow 1 walks a new member from download to Day 1.

Flow 1 — New member onboarding experience

Step 1
Launch & account creation

Alex downloads the app and registers securely using one of two paths:

Google Email / Password
  • Profile photo upload — Alex can add a profile picture during account creation.
  • Forgot Password — standard self-service recovery ensures seamless account access.
  • Guest mode is disabled — deliberately, to protect community safety and preserve streak data.
Step 2
Language preference selection

Alex selects English, with full native support for Spanish. The interface, lectionary readings, and AI responses all switch immediately.

Step 3
Parish selection via geolocation map (Parish Finder)

With GPS enabled, the interactive map shows pins for nearby congregations so Alex can select his parish.

Step 4
State of Life (Life Role track) selection

The app asks: “Where do you spend the majority of your energy?”

💼 Worker / Professional 👶 Parent / Caregiver 🎓 Student / Youth 🕰️ Senior Citizen 🌱 Beginner

Alex selects Office Worker / Professional. The daily habits immediately adapt to workplace stress and ethical leadership. (The five short labels used across the app are Worker, Parent, Student, Senior, Beginner.)

Step 5
Setting the rhythm (notification windows)

Alex sets their commute reminder for 7:30 AM and their evening wind-down for 8:30 PM.

Step 6
Arrival on dashboard

Day 1 opens with a reassuring message of grace: “Take it one day at a time. This is not a test.”

The 24-hour walk of faith

A. The daily chronological journey

Notification times are the ones Alex chose during onboarding (Flow 1 Step 5) — in this story, 7:30 AM commute and 8:30 PM evening wind-down.

7:30 AM
Morning Reminder

Push notification arrives at Alex's set morning window:

Push · Morning

Tapping the notification deep-links directly into the audio player.

7:45 AM
Morning commute — the Bishop's audio + Spotify
  • Unified sticky / persistent media player: Alex listens to the 3-minute Bishop reflection while scrolling through the day's tasks. He can download the audio from the player itself for offline listening.
  • 🎧 Spotify Companion Link: below the audio player, a button reads [ 🎵 Listen to our curated playlist on Spotify ]. Tapping it smoothly opens the Synod's curated playlist in the Spotify app for the rest of the commute.
12:30 PM
Midday break — Daily Scripture Reader & Habits
  • NRSVUE Scripture Reader: Alex reads the daily assigned lectionary excerpt (font size adjustable with [A− / A+]). The reading itself is a ~2-minute lesson.
  • 5 Daily Habits are always available — tailored specifically for Alex's Life Role as an Office Worker. In today's story, Alex happens to check off 2 workplace habits at midday (enough to meet the Rule of Two), but he can check all 5.
8:30 PM
Evening Reminder → check-in

Push notification arrives at Alex's set evening window:

Push · Evening

🌙 Evening Wind-Down — Take 30 seconds to reflect. How was your day?

  • Tapping the notification deep-links directly to the check-in screen.
  • The evening check-in window runs from 7:00 PM to 3:59 AM.
  • Alex selects a mood status — 🟢 Good / 🟡 Tough / ⚪ Rest — and optionally writes a Private 1-Sentence Note (strict 280-character cap / ~50 words).
3:59 AM
Day officially closes

Grace-based streak evaluation updates. Notes & checks remain encrypted on the phone for the current month.

B. The “No-Guilt” Streak Engine

Implements the mandate in Section 5 (p. 6) for “streak logic to encourage consistency without shaming.” Four rules — how a day is scored, then how the calendar and month keep score without guilt.

🟢 Yes → Day Complete

Rule of Two: there are always 5 habits on the Disciple's Menu. Checking any 2 — or finishing the 2-minute daily reading — earns the 🟢 Complete badge and Monthly total +1. Alex may check all 5; “2” is the floor that marks success, not a ceiling.

Met Rule of Two? Busy day / sickness is OK
🍃 No → Rest Day

Zero guilt, no penalty. The calendar shows a peaceful green leaf (🍃) — never a red X or broken chain.

The 4:00 AM Cutoff

The operational day runs from 4:00 AM to 3:59 AM. Shift workers and night owls reflecting at 1:15 AM receive full credit for the day just completed.

Monthly consistency over fragile chains

Progress is measured cumulatively — e.g. “22 / 25 Days Active this Month (88% Consistency)” — eliminating the anxiety of starting from zero.

Act III · Flow 3

The Gathering — small groups & Anchor accountability

Fulfils Section 6: Basic Fraternity & Accountability (p. 6). Groups are formed with a simple 6-character code. The group dashboard is an offline trigger: status dots prompt a real phone call or lunch — not an in-app chat feed.

Group formation & the Anchor role

  • Groups are strictly capped at 3 to 7 people maximum to maintain intimacy and trust.
  • The Group Anchor (Marcus) acts as the spiritual facilitator with administrative oversight:
  • Pinned “Thought of the Day” — Marcus posts a short reflection (max 200 characters) pinned at the top of the dashboard. Anchor-only [ ✏️ Edit Message ].
  • Roster moderation — Marcus can remove any member via a [⋮] menu to reopen invite slots under the 7-person limit.

The 6-character code-only join flow

Groups are created and joined strictly via a simple 6-character code — no complex web links or redirects.

How the Creator (Anchor) forms a group

  • In the Community tab, taps “Create Small Group”.
  • Enters a group name.
  • The app assigns them as the Anchor and generates a unique 6-character code (e.g. CAP-789).
  • The Anchor shares the code with up to 6 friends (text, word of mouth — whatever is easiest).

How members join

  • In the Community tab, taps “Join with Code”.
  • Enters the code (e.g. CAP-789).
  • Sees a confirmation modal: “Join 'Capitol Hill Cohort' (Anchor: Marcus)?”
  • Confirms and is added to the group immediately.

Guardrails & rules

7-member hard cap

If an 8th person enters the code, entry is blocked: “This group has reached its maximum capacity of 7 members.”

Single active group

Users can belong to only one active group at a time.

Lock / Regenerate code

The Anchor can tap “Lock Group” to close entry once full, or “Regenerate Code” to invalidate an old code.

The Group Dashboard (The Offline Trigger)

  • Daily member status dots show who has checked in (🟢), who needs support (🟡), and who hasn't checked in (⚪).
  • Those dots are the cue to reach out offline — a phone call or lunch — not an in-app chat feed.
  • Private evening notes are never visible here; only the mood / status signal is shared.

Sunday Weekly Review Form & Feed

Unlocks every Sunday at 8:00 AM with 3 structured questions:

  • Where did I struggle this week?
  • Where did I experience gratitude or God's grace?
  • What is my spiritual focus for next week?
7-Day Visibility: submitted answers remain visible on the group dashboard all week (the weekly review feed), giving group members meaningful topics for Sunday fellowship.

Act IV · Flow 4

The Safe Companion — the Smart AI Guide

Fulfils Section 4: The “Digital Catechist” Lutheran AI Assistant (p. 5–6). Zero-hallucination Q&A — Alex asks a theological question; the AI searches only approved Synod PDFs & the Catechism with guardrails.

🔒 Fenced AI Knowledge Base (RAG Architecture)

The AI does not browse the public internet. It strictly queries verified PDF documents uploaded by the Synod theological staff — including Luther's Small Catechism, Synod Constitution/Bylaws, and active Social Statements / pastoral letters.

🛡️ Theological Guardrails

If Alex asks an out-of-scope question (e.g. political voting advice or stock tips), the AI politely declines: “I am only trained to assist you with our community guidelines, Lutheran teachings, and daily reflections.”

🌐 Cross-Lingual Intelligence

If Synod staff uploads a Social Statement in English, and a native Spanish speaker asks a question in Spanish, the AI reads the English theology and answers in natural, fluent Spanish.
